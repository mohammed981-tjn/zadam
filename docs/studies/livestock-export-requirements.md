# Pre-export readiness: veterinary and paperwork requirements for exporting live sheep, goats, camels and cattle from Sudan (status to October 2026)

> Research notes compiled 20 Oct 2026 for Sudagri's "pre-export readiness checklist".
>
> **Labels used on every item**
> - **[OFFICIAL]**: text published by a government, a central bank, an official gazette or an intergovernmental standard-setter (WOAH). Examples: a law on FAOLEX, the Saudi gazette *Umm al-Qura*, a CBOS circular, a MEWA PDF.
> - **[OFFICIAL via SUNA]**: an official's statement carried by the Sudan News Agency, which is the state agency. It reports an official position but is not the legal text.
> - **[NEWS]**: press reports, including officials quoted by private media. **[NEWS-opinion]** marks a column.
> - **[RESEARCH]**: ILRI, IGAD/ICPALD, FAO, WOAH PVS mission reports and peer-reviewed papers.
> - **[NGO]**: reports by non-governmental organisations.
> - **CURRENT** means the source dates from 2024–2026. **OLDER** means it is from before 2024 and may be outdated.
>
> **Quoting:** quotes keep the source's own language, with a short English gloss in brackets. Where an Arabic PDF extracted badly, I quote the readable text only.
>
> **Gaps:** where nothing was found, the item says **"not found"**. I did not estimate any number.

## 0. Key findings at a glance

1. **The legal gatekeeper in Sudan is the veterinary authority, not the trader.**
   - Sudan's *Exports and Imports of Live Animals and Meat (Veterinary Quarantine) Act, 2004* bars export animals from entering inspection/vaccination centres or quarantines without (a) a health certificate from the state of origin and (b) the "way form" from the inspection and inoculation centre.
   - Only the competent authority issues the international health certificate. [OFFICIAL, OLDER but in force as far as found]
2. **Saudi Arabia's newest official text (Umm al-Qura, 2 Oct 2026) sets Sudan-specific conditions:**
   - animals collected from areas with no recorded epidemic disease, and inspected visually before entering quarantine;
   - **at least 21 days' quarantine, starting after the import permit is issued**;
   - spraying against external parasites, **numbering of all animals**, and vector control in quarantine;
   - **RVF vaccination with Smith Burn vaccine** (South African-made), plus PPR and pox vaccination where a national programme exists;
   - lab tests: **brucellosis (Rose Bengal), FMD (3ABC ELISA), RVF competition ELISA on every animal with immunity ≥40%, and bluetongue (c-ELISA)**, with all results attached to the health certificate;
   - rejected consignments go back to Sudan. [OFFICIAL, CURRENT]
3. **Saudi "standard weights" (MEWA, applied from 7 Mar 2019):**
   - Sawakni/Awassi sheep 35 kg; Barbari sheep 25 kg; goats 20 kg; cattle 280 kg; camels 200 kg.
   - Under-weight animals require the importer to bring in enough feed to reach the standard weight. [NEWS reporting MEWA, OLDER]
   - A 2025 news report says Sudanese lambs under 35 kg were still being shipped. [NEWS, CURRENT]
4. **Females:** the 21 July 2019 decision (TMC Decision 285/2019) cancelled all permissions to export females of any species. A 2022 minister's statement and 2025 exporter statements treat the ban as still in force. [OFFICIAL via SUNA + NEWS]
5. **Banking paperwork:**
   - CBOS Policy Circular 1/2024 (6 Mar 2024): **all live animals (camels, cattle, sheep, goats…) may be exported only by advance payment or sight letter of credit**, across all land, sea and air crossings. [OFFICIAL, CURRENT]
   - CBOS Circular 1/2025 (21 Jan 2025), known only from a Chinese embassy translation: keeps that rule, requires a valid exporter registration and electronic EX forms through IMEX, and **forbids banks to process exports for natural persons (except border trade)**. In practice, a herder sells to a registered exporter. [OFFICIAL text not retrieved; secondary translation, CURRENT]
6. **Egypt:**
   - Cattle from Sudan are **slaughter-only**, killed in abattoirs at the quarantine premises.
   - Camels go through Abu Simbel or Shalateen and may then go to markets.
   - Reported quarantine lengths conflict (2–3 days for camels at Abu Simbel; 14 days at Shalateen; 21 days pre-shipment plus 7 or 28 days after arrival for cattle). [RESEARCH/NEWS/OFFICIAL, mixed dates]
7. **Rejections:**
   - ILRI 2009 confirms **30.7%** of animals offered for export were rejected inside Sudan's chain in 1997–2005, and **2.1%** at Jeddah.
   - Recorded causes: brucellosis, lymph-node swelling/abscess, mange; also emaciation, diarrhoea and pox at Port Sudan in 2005.
   - Saudi Arabia returns whole ships. In 2024 this happened for **sheep pox** (14,354 sheep, June 2024) and **low RVF vaccination** (9,837 head, April 2024). [RESEARCH + NEWS]

## 1. Sudan's own export rules (Ministry of Animal Resources and Fisheries / General Directorate of Quarantine and Meat Hygiene)

### 1.1 Legal basis

- **[OFFICIAL, OLDER, in force as far as found]** *The Exports and Imports of Live Animals and Meat (Veterinary Quarantine) Act, 2004* (Act No. 35 of 2004, dated 29/12/2004). English text on FAOLEX: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/sud189344E.pdf
  - s.6: "No export animals shall be entered into to the centre of inspection, inoculation, and veterinary quarantines and exports abattoirs unless they satisfy the health conditions and accompanied by the following documents: (a) The validated health corticated [certificate] from the state of origin; (b) The way form from the centre of inoculation and inspection."
  - Definition: "'Way form', mean such certificate as may be extracted from the competent authority to show the health condition of the animal and inoculation against epidemic diseases" (a document showing health status and vaccinations).
  - s.7: "The competent authority shall issue the international health certificates for the exports animals…" (only the competent authority, the Ministry Undersecretary or his delegate, issues export health certificates).
  - s.4/5: "The Minister, by an order… may specifies the health condtions for exports of live animals" (health conditions are set by ministerial order). **The current ministerial order itself: not found.**
  - s.11: quarantine periods are set "by a of general declaration" (by general declaration). **The current declaration: not found.**
  - s.12: "The owner shall be responsible for the animals… during the veterinary quarantine period" (feed, water and care during quarantine are the owner's responsibility).

### 1.5 Laboratory tests

- **KSA 2026** [OFFICIAL, CURRENT] (Umm al-Qura; quoted in §2.1): Rose Bengal for brucellosis; 3ABC ELISA for FMD; RVF competition ELISA multi-species (ID.VET) on **all** animals with ≥40% immunity; BT competition ELISA; "يجب إرفاق جميع نتائج الفحص المخبري مع الشهادة الصحية البيطرية في كل إرسالية" (attach all lab results to the health certificate for every consignment).
- **ICPALD c.2019** [RESEARCH, OLDER]: "Laboratory examination for FMD strains (using 3ABC-ELISA) should be carried out during 30 days quarantine period"; "100% test against Brucellosis".
- **Practice c.2005** [RESEARCH, OLDER], Univ. of Khartoum vet journal, n.d.: https://onlinejournals.uofk.edu/vet/en/article/download/1173/1045
  - "The importing country`s veterinary administration requires four certificates: Veterinary health certificate for live animals, Certificate of origin and existence, Certificate of Foot and Mouth disease examination and Certificate of Brucella test."
  - Samples from Kadaro go to Soba for a "serum agglutination test (Rose Bengal)… but in the random test of Brucella… done in Jeddah there are still positive results which lead to rejection of the whole shipment".
- **Jeddah immunity sampling, 2020** [OFFICIAL via SUNA, OLDER]: a random sample of 2% of each shipment; Sudanese officials proposed raising it to 10%: "زيادة نسبة العينة العشوائية التي تفحص من ٢ في المائة الي ١٠ في المائة" (Alnilin, 27 Aug 2020).

### 2.3 What a health certificate must state; rejection rules

- [Government report describing MEWA rules, CURRENT] USDA FAIRS Saudi Arabia, 27 Jun 2025 (URL in §1.7).
- The certificate must confirm:
  - animals are "epidemic free and infectious disease free";
  - "Date of the vaccinations specified by the Ministry upon application for the import license";
  - "Veterinary inspection for all imported animals within 24 hours prior to shipping".
- Direct shipment "with no anchorage or call to any other port" banned by KSA.
- Rejection rules:
  - "The whole shipment shall be rejected if it is infected with any of the diseases listed in table 1". Table 1 includes FMD, PPR, LSD, RVF, bluetongue, sheep and goat pox, CBPP, **CCPP**, anthrax, **brucellosis**, TB, pasteurellosis, heartwater, among others.
  - For Table 2 diseases (e.g. mange, ticks, CLA, orf, internal parasites), the shipment may be quarantined if infection "does not exceed 10 percent of the random sample"; above 10% it is refused.
- [NEWS quoting MEWA, OLDER] Al-Watan, 16 Oct 2019: https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1025412
  - "عند ثبوت إصابة رأس واحد بالمرض ترفض كامل الإرسالية، دون إنزالها" (if one head is proven infected, the whole consignment is rejected without unloading).

## 6. WOAH Terrestrial Code: general requirements for live-animal export certificates (brief)

[OFFICIAL standard] WOAH Terrestrial Code ch. 5.2 "Certification procedures" (2024 edition page): https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_certification_procedures.htm

- **Certifying veterinarians should:**
  - "be authorised by the Veterinary Authority of the exporting country to sign international veterinary certificates";
  - "only certify matters that are within their own knowledge at the time of signing… or that have been separately attested by another competent party";
  - "have no conflict of interest in the commercial aspects of the animals… and be independent from the commercial parties".
- **Certificates:**
  - "designed so as to minimize the potential for fraud including use of a unique identification number";
  - signature plus official stamp, in a colour different from the print;
  - "should require appropriate identification of animals";
  - "should not be amended except by deletions which should be signed and stamped";
  - "Only original certificates are acceptable".
  - Electronic certificates may be exchanged directly between Veterinary Authorities, with equivalent safeguards.
- **Not certifiable** (art. 5.2.1): "It is unacceptable to ask for certification for events which will take place after the document is signed when these events are not under the direct control and supervision of the signing veterinarian."
- **Model certificate** (ch. 5.10, art. 5.10.2), Spanish current edition: https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/esp/Health_standards/tahc/current/es_chapitre_certif_live_animals.htm
  - Fields include consignor and consignee, place of origin, place of loading, departure date, means of transport, HS code, quantity, and intended use (breeding / slaughter…);
  - box I.24 covers species, identification system, identification numbers, age and sex.
- **RVF** (ch. 8.15): see §1.4.

## 8. Checklist candidates (each traceable; for the product team, not legal advice)

These items only restate the sourced requirements above.

1. **Animal is male.** Female export is banned for all species. Source: TMC Decision 285/2019 (§1.8).
2. **Species and destination check.** Saudi conditions for Sudan (Umm al-Qura 2026, §2.1); Egypt cattle are slaughter-only (§3).
3. **Weight at or above the MEWA standard weight** for the destination: Sawakni sheep 35 kg; goats 20 kg; cattle 280 kg; camels 200 kg (§1.8; label as "MEWA 2019 standard weights").
4. **Age for Hajj/Adahi buyers:** sheep ≥6 months; goats ≥1 year; cattle ≥2 years; camels ≥5 years; no disqualifying defects; no tail docking; ear cut ≤1/3 (§1.8, §2.5).
5. **Visible health issues that commonly cause rejection:**
   - abscess or swollen lymph nodes;
   - mange or skin lesions;
   - emaciation;
   - diarrhoea;
   - pox lesions;
   - uncastrated male where castration is expected.

   Use these as *prompts to see a vet*, not as a diagnosis (§5).
6. **Vaccination record** from a veterinarian or inspection centre. Fields to record (§1.4):
   - date;
   - vaccine;
   - batch;
   - who gave it;
   - for KSA: RVF (Smith Burn), PPR and pox.
7. **Origin documents:** a health certificate from the state of origin is needed to enter an inspection & vaccination centre (2004 Act s.6).
8. **Buyer is a registered exporter.** Banks do not process exports for natural persons except border trade. Payment is advance or sight L/C only (§1.7).
9. **Quarantine time and cost planning:** ≥21 days for KSA after the permit date; owner pays feed and care (2004 Act s.12; §1.3).

Show each item with its source, its date and a "last verified" date. Mark items that only news reports as "reported".

## 9. What a platform may and may not claim

**Basis**
- Sudan's 2004 Act gives the competent authority alone the power to issue international health certificates and export seals (ss.7–8).
- Entry to quarantine requires the state-of-origin certificate and the inspection-centre "way form" (s.6).
- WOAH ch. 5.2 requires certifying veterinarians to be authorised by the Veterinary Authority, independent of commercial parties, and to certify only what they know at signing.
- Saudi Arabia accepts only original official certificates with lab results attached, and decides at the port (§2.1, §2.3).
- CBOS rules route the export transaction through registered exporters and banks (§1.7).

**The platform MAY:**
- Help a herder or trader *prepare*:
  - keep a record of vaccinations and treatments entered by the herder or a vet, with photos of receipts and cards;
  - track animal sex, age (by teeth) and weight against the published thresholds;
  - list which documents an exporter or inspection centre will ask for;
  - show which quarantines or centres were reported operating, and when.
- Show each requirement with its source link and date, and say clearly when a requirement comes only from news or is older than 2024.
- Flag likely problems, e.g. "female: cannot be exported", "below the MEWA 35 kg standard weight", "no RVF vaccination recorded", "visible abscess: see a vet". Phrase these as reasons to consult the veterinary authority.
- Connect herders to registered exporters, licensed vets and inspection & vaccination centres.
- Use wording such as "جاهز للعرض على مركز التفتيش والتحقين" (ready to present to an inspection and vaccination centre) or "مستوفٍ لقائمة التحضير" (meets the preparation checklist).

**The platform MAY NOT:**
- Say or imply that an animal is "certified", "healthy", "disease-free", "vaccinated per protocol", "RVF-immune ≥40%" or "export-approved". Only the veterinary authority's tests and certificates establish these.
- Issue, fill in, alter or imitate a health certificate, way form, seal, ear tag series or quarantine record.
- Claim compliance with the Saudi, Egyptian or any other import protocol, or guarantee acceptance at Jeddah, Abu Simbel or any port. Importing authorities reject whole consignments at their own discretion (§2.3, §5).
- Present lab-type results (brucellosis, FMD 3ABC, RVF ELISA) unless they come from an official or accredited laboratory, and then only as that laboratory's result.
- Advise on getting around the female export ban, the weight rules or the payment rules, or describe smuggling routes as options.
- Present older (pre-2024) or news-only requirements as current law without the label.
